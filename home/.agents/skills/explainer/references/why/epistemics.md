# Epistemics

Code shows what it does, not why it exists. Every claim in a why-answer sits in one tier, and its phrasing matches the tier.

| tier | evidence | phrasing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | An author wrote the reason: a PR body, ticket, comment, design doc. | "This exists because X," with the citation. |
| Supported | Several indirect sources converge; none states it. | "The evidence points to X: A, B, C." |
| Inferred | Your reading of context, unsupported by the record. | "Given A and B, X seems likely because C." |
| Speculative | Plausible, but other explanations fit equally well. | "One possibility is X; we found no evidence." |
| Unknown | You looked and found nothing. | "We searched A, B, C for D and found no rationale." |

- "because", "was designed to", "fixes", and "the team decided" claim Direct or Supported evidence. Use them only next to a citation.
- Don't retrofit a clean rationale onto messy history, and don't read a consistent pattern as intentional when it may be copy-paste.
- When sources contradict, show both with citations. Don't pick the tidier story.
- An honest "we don't know" is a valuable answer. An answer with no gaps listed is suspicious.
